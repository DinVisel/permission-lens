# PL-7710-004 — Unrecognized caveat enforcer

**Severity:** high
**Confidence:** certain
**Needs chain data:** no

## Condition

At least one caveat's `enforcer` address isn't in the registry.

## Why it matters (LEARNING.md §7.3)

An enforcer is identified purely by its address — there's no way to inspect
"what a caveat does" other than knowing what code lives at that address. A
malicious request can include a caveat that *looks* restrictive (a
plausible-sounding `terms` blob) at an address that enforces nothing at
all, or something different from what it appears to. This decoder follows
the only safe rule: **an unrecognized enforcer is treated as providing no
restriction whatsoever**, exactly as if that caveat weren't there.

## What to do

Don't trust the apparent restriction from an unrecognized caveat. Evaluate
the delegation as if that caveat is absent — see whether the *remaining*
recognized caveats (if any) are enough to make this delegation acceptable.
