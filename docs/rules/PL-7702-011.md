# PL-7702-011 — Matches a sweeper pattern

**Severity:** critical
**Confidence:** heuristic
**Needs chain data:** yes (the delegate's bytecode)

## Condition

The delegate's bytecode matches
[`crudeSweeperHeuristic`](../../packages/core/src/bytecode/sweeper-heuristic.ts):
a full 20-byte hardcoded address (never a trivial value like the zero
address or a compiler address-mask constant) pushed shortly before a
value-transfer-capable opcode (`CALL`/`CALLCODE`/`DELEGATECALL`/
`STATICCALL`/`SELFDESTRUCT`).

## Why it matters

This is the bulk of real-world EIP-7702 abuse (LEARNING.md §6.1): an
attacker who already has your private key delegates your account to code
that immediately forwards its balance to an address they control. The
pattern is deliberately obvious in the bytecode — the attacker doesn't need
to hide it, since by the time it runs, they already have full control.

## Why this is a heuristic, not a verdict

Plenty of legitimate contracts push a hardcoded address before a call (an
oracle, a fixed router, a treasury). `confidence: "heuristic"` reflects
that honestly — this rule exists to surface candidates for review, evidence
for the registry, or corroboration alongside other findings (a delegate
that's *also* unrecognized and *also* matches this pattern is a much
stronger signal than either alone). Per §8's "never say safe": the absence
of this finding is not evidence of safety either — it's a crude pattern
match, not an audit.

## What to do

Treat as critical until proven otherwise. Check the address in
`evidence.sweepTarget` against the registry's malicious-cluster data (see
[`GOVERNANCE.md`](../../GOVERNANCE.md)) and consider reporting it via
`tools/census` if it's new.
